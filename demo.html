Check-in

Type a VIN, get the whole vehicle

The VIN is checked for typos before it goes anywhere, then decoded against the federal database — year, make, model, trim, engine, drive. No VIN handy? Year, make and model are dropdowns, with real model lists that load from the same database.

  • Paint code prompts, with where the sticker lives on that make
  • Seventeen named colours, so the board reads "Champagne Silverado"
  • Tap what it's in for from a list built for that kind of work
The floor

Every vehicle, and what it's waiting on

One line per car: where it is, who it belongs to, what it's worth and when it's promised. Anything on hold for a part shows red. Filter to body, mechanical, service, detail, dealerships or ready-to-go.

The home screen goes further and tells you the one thing each vehicle needs from you — nobody assigned, estimate not sent, parts to order, money to collect.

Photos

Shot before you touch it

The camera opens inside the job. Shoot as many as you like, they upload in the background and they're attached to that vehicle forever. Your customer sees them as the work progresses, which stops the "any update?" calls before they start.

A before photo is what settles an argument with an adjuster six weeks later, when nobody remembers whether that scratch was already there.

Estimates

Write it once, bill it once

Labour, parts, materials and sublet, with line types that match the kind of work. Your own price book turns a full detail or an oil change into one tap at the right price. The estimate becomes the invoice — you never retype it.

  • Insurance jobs split automatically between carrier and deductible
  • Import a CCC, Mitchell or Audatex estimate straight onto the job
  • Hours priced at your rates, not the insurer's
  • Print it, text it, or email it as a proper document with your logo
Your crew

They log the work. You price it.

Technicians see only the jobs they're on. They move stages, take photos, write up cause and correction, and log what they did — picked from your price book at your prices, or as hours with a description. They never see another person's pay, and never set a price.

Schedules take a minute a week, and copying last week's is one tap.

Customers and dealerships

They can see it themselves

A private customer taps a link and watches their car move through your shop — no account, no password. A dealership gets a real login and sees every vehicle they have with you, sends new ones in, adds their own stock numbers, and approves estimates.

There's a public request link too. Post it, text it, print it on a card — someone fills in their vehicle and what's wrong, and it lands on your floor with photos attached.

The money

Where it came from, where it went

Billed, collected and still owed. What's left after crew pay and card fees. Labour against parts against materials. Which clients are worth the most and which vehicles sit the longest. Every figure exports to a spreadsheet.

In January, contractor forms are a button — everyone who crossed the threshold, flagged if their details are missing, in a file your filing service accepts.
