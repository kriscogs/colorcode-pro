Customers and dealerships

Two kinds of client, handled differently

A private customer is a name, a phone number and a car. Add them in fifteen seconds, or let them add themselves. They tap a link and watch their vehicle move through your shop — no account, no password, nothing to remember.

A dealership is a business with its own rate, its own terms, several vehicles and several people. They get real logins, see everything they have with you, and send you more without picking up the phone.

  • Your own labour rate and payment terms per dealership
  • Their stock numbers alongside your RO numbers
  • Archive a client and every job of theirs stays intact

Your price book

What you sell, priced once

A full detail, an oil change, a bumper respray. Each service carries what's included, how long it takes, and a price — one flat figure, or one per vehicle size if that's how you charge.

On an estimate it's a single tap at the right price. Fill in the minutes and it tells you what a job actually earns per hour, which is how you find out your SUV pricing isn't worth doing.

  • Vehicle sizes set once, used by every service
  • Shop supplies and disposal fees, flat or a capped percentage
  • Your crew adds services at your prices, never their own

Parts

Everything on order, in one place

Every part waiting across every job, grouped by who you buy from. Mark a whole group ordered and they share a PO number, so you chase one order instead of six parts.

Each supplier block pulls their phone number, account number and website from your Resources list. Copy the order gives you a clean list with the VIN and paint code attached — what the counter asks for anyway.

Your crew

Everyone on it, at no extra cost

Invite a technician and they pick their own password. From then on they see the jobs they're on and nothing else — no client list, no invoices, no other person's pay.

They log what they did as they go: a service from your price book at your price, or hours with a description. That's what their pay is worked out from, and it means nobody is reconstructing a day at five o'clock.

  • Paid hourly, on a percentage, or a flat amount per job
  • They fill in their own address and tax classification — you never can, and never see a bank number
  • Remove someone and their login dies, but their name stays on finished work

The second half is for your people rather than your suppliers: upload your handbook, benefits summary, enrolment forms and safety sheets. Everyone can open them. Nobody has to ask you for a copy.
