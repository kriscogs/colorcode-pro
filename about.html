Why this exists

My father could fix anything except the paperwork.

He was a body man. He had the kind of talent you can't teach — he could look at a crumpled quarter panel and see the shape it used to be. People brought him cars from two towns over because he made them right, and because he was honest about what they needed.

What he couldn't do was the office. The estimates went on whatever paper was nearby. The invoices went out when he remembered. Money came in and money went out and somewhere in the middle was a shoebox. Every year the paperwork got a little further behind, and every year it got a little harder to face.

He never filed his taxes.

Not out of defiance. It was just one more thing at the end of a long day, and then it was two years, and then it was too big to start. That's how it happens to people who are good with their hands and tired by six o'clock.

When he was diagnosed with stage 4 lung cancer, he applied for Social Security disability. He was denied. You qualify on the work you've reported, and he had decades of work with almost nothing on the record. A lifetime of it, and none of it counted.

He worked his whole life and the system had no idea he'd done it.

I've thought about that a great deal since. Not about the money — about the fact that everything he built disappeared because the recording of it was too hard. There was no ledger, no history, nothing to hand anyone. The work was real. The evidence wasn't.

What I built instead

Shopfloor Pros is the thing I wish had been sitting on his bench. Not accounting software — he'd never have opened accounting software. Something that keeps the record as a by-product of doing the work.

Check a vehicle in and it's on the record. Take a photo and it's attached to that job forever. Write the estimate once and it becomes the invoice. Mark a payment and it lands in a report you can hand an accountant in January without a shoebox in sight.

Nothing about it asks you to sit down at the end of the day and catch up. That's the whole design. The paperwork happens while your hands are already on the car.

Why it's built the way it is

  • Every job is dated and kept. Years from now you can show what you did, when, for whom, and what you were paid. That's the record he never had.
  • It nags about money, gently. The home screen tells you what's unbilled and what's unpaid, every day, before it becomes a year.
  • Year-end takes an afternoon, not a weekend. Contractor forms, income reports and a full export are all a button.
  • Your crew are on the record too. Hours, pay, and the tax classification that decides what lands in their mailbox in January. Nobody should be invisible.
  • Your data is yours. Export everything, any time, in one click. If you ever leave, you leave with all of it.

Who it's for

Shops of any size. Body, mechanical, service and detail. A one-man operation working out of a two-bay garage, or a floor with a dozen techs and three dealership accounts. What they have in common is people who are excellent at the work and would rather not think about the rest of it.

If that's you, I'd like you to have this. And if you know a shop where the office is a shoebox — please tell them. That's the whole point.

For my father. He built a legacy with his hands and had nothing to show for it on paper. This is so the next one does.
